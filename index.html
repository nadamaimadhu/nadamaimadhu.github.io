About Me

The onset of an aneurysm in a family friend, through the strain on their family caused by its rupture, initiated my nascent interest in neurological disorders. Learning about treatment methods, such as endovascular coiling, subsequently catalyzed my attraction to the medical field by way of their ingenuity. Consequently, I chose to pursue a medical-centric education by joining my North Carolina high school’s Medical Bio-Science Academy. I was thereafter exposed to the etiology of chronic disorders such as Diabetes and Alzheimer's by taking courses in Health Science that taught overviews of organ systems and their respective disorders. By way of this, I became aware of the significance of early intervention for chronic disorders by learning that fighting them early on slowed disease progression. Thusly, I had the opportunity to present to elementary school students in Raleigh about the implications of Type 2 Diabetes through an interactive and engaging overview of the disorder. My interest in chronic illness was subsequently exacerbated by interacting with seniors with memory challenges while facilitating recreational activities for residents at Wake Assisted Living Memory Care. From this, I deepened my interest in fighting chronic disorders by experiencing firsthand the challenges of living with neurodegenerative diseases.

Presently, I attend the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill as a member of the Class of 2030. I am currently pursuing a B.S in Neuroscience and plan to add the B.S.P.H of Nutrition to my course of study. Through courses in Neuroscience and Nutrition majors, I will deepen my understanding of the biochemical mechanisms behind chronic disorders such as Alzheimer's and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. Consequently, in successive semesters, I would like to use this knowledge to contribute to research at UNC by joining labs that advance treatments for chronic brain and metabolic disorders. As a result, I hope that spending time in neurodegenerative disorder or endocrinology laboratories will build my understanding of laboratory techniques needed to pursue a career involving research in various capacities. Through gaining knowledge of these technical skills, I would like to enhance my adaptability to differing lab expectations and improve my core understanding of the chronic diseases I work with.

Past UNC, I plan to apply to MD programs and eventually complete a residency in either neurology or endocrinology. I hope that thenceforth, I can continue not only researching but also implementing solutions for chronic diseases as a physician. Consequently, becoming a physician, I most anticipate the opportunity to foster long-term relationships with my patients by working to help them manage their conditions over extended periods of time. Through these relationships, I hope to stay motivated to serve my patients by keeping up to date with novel solutions for the very chronic illnesses that directly affect them.
