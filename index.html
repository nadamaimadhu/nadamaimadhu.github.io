About Me

Occasionally, between long periods of strenuous effort, there is a sense of Eureka when we understand an additional piece of the brain puzzle. May-Britt Moser, Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 2014

The onset of an aneurysm in a family friend, through the strain on their family from its rupture, initiated my nascent interest in neurological disorders. Learning about treatment methods for these disorders, such as endovascular coiling, subsequently catalyzed my attraction to medicine by way of their complexity. Consequently, I chose to pursue a medical-centric education by joining my North Carolina high school’s Medical Bio-Science concentration. I was thereafter exposed to the etiology of chronic disorders such as diabetes and Alzheimer's Disease by taking courses in Health Science that taught overviews of organ systems and their respective disorders. This led me to question: what, if any, is the causal relationship between early intervention for chronic disorders and slowed disease progression? Thusly, I presented to elementary school students in Raleigh about the implications of Type 2 Diabetes through an interactive overview of the disorder. My interest in chronic illness was subsequently heightened by interacting with seniors with memory challenges while facilitating recreational activities for residents at Wake Assisted Living Memory Care. From this, I deepened my interest in fighting chronic disorders by experiencing firsthand the challenges of living with neurodegenerative diseases.

Currently, I attend the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill as a member of the Class of 2030, where I am pursuing a B.S. in Neuroscience and plan to add the B.S.P.H. in Nutrition to my course of study. Through courses in the Neuroscience and Nutrition majors, I will deepen my understanding of the biochemical underpinnings of chronic disorders such as Alzheimer's and Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. Consequently, in successive semesters, I would like to apply this knowledge to research at UNC by joining a lab that advances treatments for chronic brain and metabolic disorders. As a result, I hope that spending time in neurodegenerative disorder or endocrinology laboratories will intensify my curiosity about chronic diseases and accordingly allow me to bring a unique perspective as a physician dedicated to combating these diseases.

After UNC, I plan to apply to MD programs and eventually complete a residency in either neurology or endocrinology. I hope that henceforth, I can continue not only conducting research but also but also implementing solutions for chronic diseases as a physician. Consequently, as a physician, I most anticipate the opportunity to foster long-term relationships with my patients by working to help them manage their conditions over extended periods of time. Through these relationships, I hope to stay motivated to serve my patients by keeping up to date with novel solutions for the very chronic illnesses that directly affect them.
