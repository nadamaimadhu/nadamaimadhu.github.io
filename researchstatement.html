Research Statement

"Occasionally, between long periods of strenuous effort, there is a sense of Eureka when we understand an additional piece of the brain puzzle." May-Britt Moser, Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 2014

Degradation of axonal transport (AT) mechanisms in non-glial cells is a vital factor in the onset of chronic neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's Disease (AD). Previous research indicates that axonal transport is impaired by amyloid-beta (Αβ) oligomers, through casein kinase 2 (CK2) activation. My future research aims to address how specific conformations of oligomers differentially impact AT. These findings could inform the development of conformation-specific drugs that target toxic oligomers, limiting AT dysfunction and subsequently slowing the progression of AD.
