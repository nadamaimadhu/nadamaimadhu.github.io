Research Statement

"Man himself is a mysterious object and the tools to probe his physiologic nature and function have developed only slowly through the millenia."" Rosalyn Yalow, Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 1977

Degradation of axonal transport (AT) mechanisms in non-glial cells is a vital factor in the onset of chronic neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's Disease (AD). Previous research asserts that axonal transport is impaired by oligomerization of peptides that stem from mutations in the APP, PSEN1, and PSEN2 genes through their increased production of AB42 proteins. My future research aims to address how specific conformations of oligomers lead to differing axonal transport failures. These findings could implicate the development of drugs that target toxic oligomeric species unique to their configuration which can limit AT dysfunction, consequently, slowing the progression of AD.
